**Abstract:**

Efficiency and human rights protection are often perceived as competing objectives within the criminal justice system, with Plea Bargaining systems exemplifying this tension. In 2016, China introduced its own version of Plea Bargaining, the Plea Leniency system, aiming to enhance criminal justice efficiency while safeguarding human rights. This study assesses the impact of China’s Plea Leniency system on criminal justice outcomes, specifically examining its ability to balance these dual objectives. Utilizing a Difference-in-Differences (DiD) model with multiple data from 95 Chinese cities over the period 2012–2019, we find that the Plea Leniency system has increased the number of individuals prosecuted by approximately 10%, primarily due to lower conviction standards rather than efficiency gains or deterrence effects. A 2.67% increase in prosecution rates further suggests that the system may prioritize efficiency at the expense of human rights protection. Despite policy measures designed to achieve a balance between efficiency and human rights, our findings indicate that the fundamental tension between these two goals persists. Moreover, further analysis suggests that the Plea Leniency system has evolved into a distorted mechanism, ultimately steering China’s criminal justice system further away from the protection of human rights.
