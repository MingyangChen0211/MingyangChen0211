**Note**: *This is not an academic paper, but just some observations.*

*I would ask you to validate the information provided in this blog on your own. Besides, different provinces or cities would have different situations, so stories here can not be generalized to other regions of China.*

***

I got to know what the education system of my hometown, a small county (*县城*) in China, is like during this summer holiday. I grew up in this county, but I had started to study in the urban area (*市区*) since I became a junior high school student. Before this summer, I had never known that there is a huge difference between the education received in the counties and in the urban area. This blog aims to introduce the education disparities in my hometown. As a social scientist, I will also show you some data and my personal experiences to illustrate my observations. 

To begin with, I need to illustrate what an urban area is and what a county is in China. Usually, in China, the county is a smaller unit than the city. For example, in the city called Quanzhou, there are two districts, Fengze and Licheng. The two districts are called the "urban area". They are richer, with better facilities and more job opportunities. The two areas have several "Key Schools" (*重点中学*), while in other counties, e.g., Yongchun, Hui'an, and Anxi, they only have one or two in each county. Counties are less developed, and the majority of the county adults are government officers or blue-collar workers. There is almost no high-salary private company white-collar staff. Usually, the No.1 middle schools (*一中*) are the "Key Schools" in the county. Even though they are all "Key Schools", students' lives and grades are totally different, not to mention other schools. And that is what I want to convey to my readers: the educational disparities in China. 

First, Key Schools in the urban area have their own supplementary materials, which are called "JiaoFu" (*教辅*). Students in the urban area would use JiaoFu and their textbooks to study. In JiaoFu, all knowledge and after-class exercises are designed for "Gaokao" (*高考*), the entrance examination to the university in China. Thus, students in urban area Key Schools can spend less time to get a higher score in Gaokao. For students from the counties, they can only utilize the textbooks and examination materials that can be bought from the bookstores. Someone may be confused: why can't the county school teachers also design their "JiaoFu"? In fact, it is too hard for them because of the following reasons (I guess): (1) fewer high-quality teachers. Urban schools would recruit excellent old county teachers with a higher salary or a promotion; For those who have just graduated from the normal universities, if they are from some famous universities, e.g., Huadong Normal University, they would definitely choose urban schools, either for their next generation's education or for their life quality. (2) Lack of money to build a high-quality Teaching-Research Group (*教研组*). Take my high school as an example: the expenditure is about [192 million](https://jyj.quanzhou.gov.cn/zwgk/czxx/bmyjsgk/202504/P020250414060697819260.pdf) per year, while for Anxi No. 1 School, it is only [64 million](http://www.fjax.gov.cn/zwgk/zfxxgkzl/zfxxgkml/czyjs/bmyjs/202404/P020240519116188823100.pdf) per year. The student numbers are similar, however. According to my own estimation, both my high school and the Anxi No.1 School have about 3000 students. Thus, each student in the urban area can enjoy more than three times the expenditures compared with students from a county. Thus, county schools only provide some JiaoFu in the third year, while for urban school students, they have special JiaoFu even in the previous two years. Second, schools in counties prefer the "HengShui mode" (*衡水模式*). This is a student management style from Hengshui, Hebei Province. It is famous for making students into a Gaokao machine. Usually in these schools, students are not allowed to wear their own clothes or bring any electronic devices, or choose their own hairstyles. Students are required to run around the playground in the morning (*跑操*; urban schools also have it, but some use the form of doing aerobics), read aloud after stepping into the classroom in the morning (*早读*), and so on. You can search for "HengShui" on TikTok, and you will know it. County schools believe that as long as students work hard and live like soldiers, they will become successful in Gaokao. But the reality is ironic: one Key School in the urban area can produce about [15 students](https://www.toutiao.com/article/7532960922269123099/?wid=1788322375027) getting admission offers from Tsinghua or Peking University, which is even more than a whole county's. Urban schools also have a large number of students going to 985 or 211 colleges, which are all very excellent universities in China, although schools in the urban area use relatively loose management. Take my undergraduate roommates as an example: 4 out of 5 of my roommates graduated from an urban key school. Opportunities for county students are rare. What is more, urban school students, if they want, can be admitted to universities in Hong Kong and Macau, sometimes with a full scholarship, using their Gaokao score, while for county students, even though they have a good Gaokao score, they do not even know that they can study abroad. Three years of HengShui mode training make them know nothing about university admissions. Some special universities held by the mainland-China government and Hong Kong universities, e.g., the CUHK(SZ), will not even promote themselves in a county, but give more opportunities to urban school students. Besides, HengShui is successful not only for its strict management, but also for the JiaoFu, as I mentioned before. But these county schools will not design their JiaoFu, but choose some apparent measures to let students live a hard life. As a comparison, urban high schools usually give students more freedom. Students can eat breakfast in the classroom, and they are not required to read aloud in the morning (although there are such regulations). They can make up and design their hairstyles. Teachers in these schools would not do things like forcing students to cut their hair in public. But in recent years, it seems that these schools have also started to imitate the HengShui mode. The two differences mentioned above contribute to such a consequence: students in the urban key schools can have a relatively happy life (under the system of Gaokao, it is hard to say that students have a "happy life", but compared with other students, they have fewer possibilities to have trauma), enjoying activities, e.g., school fairs, new year parties, full art educations and sports educations. These are not allowed in some county schools, because art and sports are considered unrelated to Gaokao, and it is a waste of time to learn them. Even in urban schools, many students would think so as well. Students lacking a full education and investing everything into Gaokao while getting a bad score would develop some psychological problems. For example, on TikTok, some students would say that "I work so hard to get into a bad college" (*起早贪黑上大专*). The gap between their hard work and the de facto achievement would make them feel stressed, according to criminological theories. You may have heard about Chinese students committing suicide, and I would argue that it is partially a result of education disparity and school management. Furthermore, after getting into universities, difficulties come to county students: they do not know how to complete a presentation, because it is not related to Gaokao; they used to live like an ascetic monk, so some of them would be addicted to smartphone games and forgo studying new things in the university, with a potential to be expelled. This is truly happening around me. I can still remember that some of my presentation group members told me that they had never done any presentations when I was in my undergraduate studies. Thus, they had to learn everything from zero. If you ask where they graduated from, the majority of those students would say that they come from rural areas or counties. The education disparity is influencing students more than we have thought. It is not only about the scores and the unhappy memories, but it will also influence students' future studies.
